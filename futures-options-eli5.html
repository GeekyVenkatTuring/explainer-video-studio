Futures & options
are price promises.

Imagine you want to buy mangoes next month. You worry their price might change.

Future = a promise

Next month:
100 mangoes at ₹10 each
BOTH MUST DO IT

You and the seller lock a price today. On the date, both sides must keep the deal.

Option = a choice

Pay ₹1 today
for the choice to buy at ₹10
BUYER CHOOSES

You pay a small premium for a choice. The buyer may use it—or walk away.

What happens next month?

Suppose the market price of a mango is now ₹16. Your locked price was ₹10.

FutureYou buy at ₹10. Nice if you wanted to buy; painful if prices had fallen instead.
Call optionYou use your right to buy at ₹10. The choice is valuable when the price rises.
If price falls to ₹7With the option, you can skip the ₹10 deal. You lose the ₹1 premium.
The grown-up warning: Futures are often traded with borrowed-like collateral called margin, so losses can grow quickly and may exceed the money first put in. Buying an option limits the buyer’s loss to the premium, but selling some options can create very large losses. These are useful risk-management tools, not easy-money machines.
Educational simplification. Exact contract sizes, expiration rules, and risks depend on the market and broker. Sources: CFTC futures basics and SEC Investor.gov options bulletin.

Options have
a clock.

A share can be held for years. An option has an expiry. As time passes, the chance of a big move before expiry can shrink—so an option buyer can lose value even when the underlying barely moves.

Two parts of an option price

Intrinsic value: the immediate value if exercised now.

Time value: the extra amount someone pays for the possibility that the price changes before expiry.

Near expiry, time is scarcer. This is why “I was right about direction” is not enough for an option buyer.

What could happen?

You buy a call. The underlying stays almost unchanged for several days. Which statement can be true?

Choose one.

Use this question before every paper trade: “How much time does my idea need, and does this contract give it enough?”

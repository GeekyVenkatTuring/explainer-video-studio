Promise
or choice?

Your first F&O skill: before looking at profit, identify who must do what.

Why this matters for your mission
F&O can look like investing because it uses familiar stocks and indices. But it is a time-limited contract with different obligations and risks.

The one idea

Future = promise

You agree today on a price for later.

Buyer and seller are both obligated. If the price moves against you, the future still exists.

Option = choice

You pay for a possible deal.

The buyer has a right, not an obligation. The seller has the obligation if the buyer uses it.

A tiny example

You expect an index to rise. An option gives you the right to buy at a fixed “strike” price. If the market falls, a call-option buyer can let it expire. The premium paid is lost; the buyer does not have to buy at the strike.

A futures buyer facing the same fall does not get to simply walk away. Losses are marked against the position and margin may need topping up.

Try it

Scenario: You pay ₹X for the right to buy an index at 25,000 before expiry. At expiry it is 24,000. What can you do?

Choose an answer.
Safety checkpoint
“Option buyer” does not mean “safe investment,” and it does not describe an option seller. The buyer can lose 100% of the premium. Futures and option-selling can create losses beyond the amount initially deposited. SEBI reported that 93% of individual equity F&O traders incurred losses in FY22–FY24; that is a historical aggregate finding, not a forecast for you.
Before the next lesson: say this aloud: “A future is an obligation; an option buyer owns a choice; an option seller owns an obligation.”

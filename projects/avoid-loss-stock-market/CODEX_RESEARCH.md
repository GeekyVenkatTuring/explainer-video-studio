**Research date:** 29 August 2026 (Saturday)  
**Price date:** 28 August 2026, the latest completed NSE session  
**Capital context:** Approximately ₹15,000; stretch target ₹30,000 in about one year

## Executive view

A 100% return in one year is a **top-decile, high-risk outcome**, not a sensible base case for any of these stocks. Even the published targets below generally imply only about 15–43% upside from the 28 August 2026 closing prices. Doubling would require unusually strong earnings surprises, a major valuation re-rating, or both—and would expose the investor to a meaningful probability of permanent loss.

All five companies are NSE-listed and are absent from the Nifty 50 and Nifty Next 50 constituent lists reviewed on 29 August 2026. Size classifications use AMFI's market-cap categorisation based on the six-month average market capitalisation to 30 June 2026, rather than an improvised rupee cutoff. Dalmia Bharat is a mid-cap; the other four are small-caps under that classification.

## 1. Kalpataru Projects International

**NSE symbol:** `KPIL`  
**Sector:** Engineering, procurement and construction—power transmission and distribution, buildings and factories, water, railways, oil and gas pipelines  
**Market cap and class:** Approximately **₹23,674 crore** at the 28 August 2026 close; **small-cap**, AMFI rank 341 at 30 June 2026.

**Observed price:** **₹1,386.30 on 28 August 2026**.

### Bull thesis

KPIL entered FY27 with a record **₹66,607-crore order book** and reported **₹6,408 crore of Q1 FY27 revenue, up 4% YoY, and ₹312 crore of PAT, up 46% YoY**. The order book provides unusually good revenue visibility for a small-cap, while a better project mix, working-capital discipline and falling finance costs could convert modest top-line growth into faster earnings growth over the next four quarters.

The rerating case is concrete rather than purely thematic: continued large transmission-and-distribution and buildings-and-factories wins, execution of the existing order book, and collection of roughly **₹1,500 crore of water-business receivables** can improve both return ratios and the market's confidence in cash conversion. At **23.03× trailing earnings versus a cited sector P/E of 30.88×**, the stock does not require an extreme multiple to reach published targets.

## My single top pick: Kalpataru Projects International (`KPIL`)

**KPIL is the best risk-adjusted high-upside candidate in this group**, not the stock most likely to double. Its record ₹66,607-crore order book provides the clearest earnings visibility; Q1 PAT growth of 46% shows that operating improvement can translate into earnings; and its 23.03× P/E is below the cited sector multiple, unlike several peers here whose rerating is already partly priced in. The realistic published-target range is roughly **15–23% upside**, with a larger outcome possible only if execution, margins and receivable collection beat expectations. The choice is not low risk: the approximately ₹1,500-crore water receivable balance and promoter pledge are material red flags, and I would not treat ₹15,000 becoming ₹30,000 in one year as a probable result. A single ₹15,000 position is concentrated; capital preservation argues for sizing modestly and accepting that the stretch target may not be reached.

## Bottom line

The best balance of visibility, valuation and catalysts is KPIL. Jubilant Ingrevia has the largest verified brokerage-target upside but greater commissioning, cyclicality and valuation risk. Wabag has strong structural demand and order visibility but weak cash conversion; KFin has the best return ratios but is expensive and currently experiencing margin dilution; Dalmia offers an integration-driven recovery at a relative discount, though present profitability and leverage are weaker.

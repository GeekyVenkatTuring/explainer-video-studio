Lesson 6 · 10 minutes

Write it before
you see the outcome.

Paper trading means recording a hypothetical trade and then watching what would happen. Its purpose is not to prove you can predict markets; it is to prove you can define risk and follow a process.

  • Idea: what exactly do I think will happen, by when?
  • Contract: underlying, expiry, strike, CE/PE, and live lot size.
  • Maximum loss: calculate it in ₹, including an allowance for charges.
  • Invalidation: what result would show the idea was wrong?
  • Exit rule: when would I close it, before expiry?
  • Review: did I follow the plan—not just did it make paper profit?

Process check

A trade made profit, but you never calculated its downside and exited only from excitement. Is it a successful learning trade?

Choose one.
Real-money gate: Do not move from paper trading to real money based on a short winning streak. First complete at least 20 documented paper trades, explain every maximum loss, and review the journal for rule-following. F&O is not a substitute for diversified, long-term investing.
